## Instagram hit-list (open these, leave a genuine comment)

Spaced out through the day, a few at a time, not all at once. The comment is a starting point, tweak it to fit the actual post.

**1. Creators in interabled relationships or disabled people posting openly about their dating history on mainstream apps**
_These accounts put the disclosure-then-disappear pattern in plain sight, which backs up the whole reason this account exists to argue against silence on the subject_
> That moment the replies just stop after disability comes up in the bio or the chat isn't rare, disabled daters have been naming that exact drop off for years and mainstream apps still pretend it's a coincidence.

**2. Manchester based access or nightlife accounts covering Gay Village venues, club nights or Pride events**
_Direct line to the nightlife exclusion theme, particularly wherever an event brands itself inclusive without checking who can physically get in_
> An 'inclusive' night held somewhere a wheelchair user cannot get through the front door isn't inclusive, it's a headline event with a step nobody wants to mention out loud.

**3. #disabilitydating posts where someone is venting about being matched, chatted up and then ghosted on a mainstream app**
_This hashtag is where the disclosure pattern gets described first hand, in the daters' own words rather than a third party summary_
> Match, chat, disappear the second disability is mentioned, disabled daters have been describing this exact sequence for years and it keeps getting rebranded as bad luck.

**4. #wheelchairlife posts documenting an access fail at a venue, bar or event, steps with no ramp, no step free entrance**
_Grounds the venue exclusion argument in a real, specific access fail rather than a general complaint_
> Another night out that ends at a flight of steps nobody thought to check, and the venue still gets called welcoming to everyone.

**5. Access campaigners or heritage/accessibility consultants posting about a listed building refusing ramps or lifts**
_Directly tests the historic building myth this account holds a hard line on_
> Somehow every 'you can't touch a listed building' claim survives right up until a ramp actually goes in, refusing reasonable access has never been about the bricks.

**6. #accessibility posts celebrating a genuine access win, step free entrance added, a ramp fitted, a venue that actually checked before promising access**
_Where the celebration half of this account's position lives, proper recognition when access gets built properly_
> Step free entrance fitted and it actually works on the day, this is what every venue claiming to be accessible should have done from the start.
