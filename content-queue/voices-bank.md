# Able2Love — Voices Bank

Anonymised, rephrased real experiences disabled people shared about dating, ready to drop into
posts, testimonials and outreach. Sourced from the three Reddit threads (see
`Documents/Able2Love-Reddit-Insights.md`), then reworded into representative composites.

**The rules these all follow (don't break them):**
- No names, no handles, no identifying detail — attribution is always generic ("A wheelchair user").
- Rephrased, never lifted word-for-word.
- Never dressed up as a statistic or a percentage.
- Never implies these people use Able2Love — they're describing dating in general.
- Public use is framed honestly as *real experiences disabled people shared*, not app reviews.

The machine-readable version the bots pull from lives at `automation/lib/voices-pool.mjs`.
⭐ = strong enough to stand alone as a quote/testimonial card. 🔒 = safety-sensitive, only ever
use with a supportive frame, never as shock.

---

## "Is it the disability, or me?"
- ⭐ *"For years I couldn't work out if it was the disability or just me. It was neither. It was other people deciding who I was before I'd said a word."* — a wheelchair user
- *"Finding someone doesn't mean you did it right, and not finding someone doesn't mean you did it wrong. It's mostly luck and timing. Nobody tells you that."* — a disabled man

## Self-esteem was the real barrier
- ⭐ *"The thing that nearly stopped me wasn't my body. It was being convinced I was too much to ask anyone to take on."* — a disabled man, now married
- *"I spent my twenties assuming I was a dealbreaker, so I barely tried. The disability was never the problem. My own head was."* — a disabled man in his 40s

## Apps fail; real life works
- *"Every good thing happened away from the apps. Through a hobby, through friends, through people who already knew I was more than a diagnosis."* — a disabled man
- *"The cold apps are rough for everyone, and worse when the first thing they see is the chair instead of you."* — a wheelchair user

## The disclosure dilemma
- ⭐ *"The worst part isn't telling someone. It's the silence right after, while you wait to find out if that was the end of it."* — someone with an invisible illness
- *"A visible disability takes away your choice of when to say anything. It's decided for you the second they look up."* — a wheelchair user

## Seen as a burden, or a utility
- *"As a disabled man you stop being seen as a date at all. You're either useful or you're invisible."* — a disabled man
- *"People want easy now. You can feel them deciding whether you'd be hard work before they've even asked your name."* — a disabled dater

## Novelty, fetishisation, safety 🔒
- 🔒 *"I've had men get a bit too interested in the idea that I might need help. That's when I block them. For disabled women, safety isn't a nice-to-have, it's the whole thing."* — a disabled woman
- ⭐🔒 *"I just want to be a person. Not someone's curiosity, and not someone's good deed."* — a disabled dater

## The situationship / ghosting wound (your own core pain)
- ⭐ *"They'd stay for weeks, act completely into it, then disappear the moment they got what they were actually after."* — a disabled dater
- *"The ghosting isn't about you. It's people running into the wrong people. It just stings more when you're already braced for it."* — a disabled dater

## Being seen as your whole self
- ⭐ *"To me the wheelchair is just how I get around. To everyone else it somehow became my entire personality."* — a wheelchair user
- *"I watched women get treated like women while I got treated like a thing, doing nothing different. That's the bit that breaks you."* — a disabled woman

## It does work out (the hope)
- ⭐ *"It came together the moment I stopped chasing and started believing I was worth staying for. We've been together years now."* — a disabled dater, years into a relationship
- *"I used to think the disability was the problem. Mostly it was me. Once I sorted that out, I loved and was loved like anyone else."* — a disabled man

## Age adds nuance (honest, not an excuse)
- *"Some of it is just getting older, everyone's world shrinks after school and uni. But don't let anyone tell you the ableism isn't real on top of that."* — a disabled dater
