# Getting Able2Love in front of people (the reach playbook)

Posting good content to a new account is like putting up a brilliant poster inside your own empty flat. Nobody walks past. This is how the posts actually reach people. Read it once; the daily bit takes 15 minutes.

## The hard truth about a new account

A brand-new account has almost no organic reach. Hashtags help a little (more on Instagram than on X), but reach for a cold account comes mostly from **you showing up in conversations other people are already having**, and from **accounts with an audience resharing you**. So the plan is three layers:

1. **The posts** (done, automated) — the daily scheduled content, now with hooks and hashtags.
2. **Engagement** (you, 15 min a day) — replying, tagging, joining threads.
3. **Borrowed audiences** — your own performer following, and creators/orgs who might reshare.

The posts alone are maybe 20% of the result. Layers 2 and 3 are the other 80%.

## Your single biggest lever: your own accounts

You are a Manchester comedy writer and performer with a real audience. **@Able2LoveApp has none yet.** So the fastest reach in the world is: **your personal/performer accounts share and talk about the app.** One post from an account with real followers beats a week of posts from a cold brand account. Repost the launch, tell the story, be a person about it. This costs nothing and it is the highest-return thing you can do this week.

## The daily 15 minutes (this is where growth happens)

Do this from the **@Able2LoveApp** account, once a day:

1. **Search 3 of our hashtags** (rotate: `#disabilitydating`, `#disabilitycommunity`, `#spoonie`, `#actuallyautistic`, `#chronicillness`, `#wheelchairlife`, `#accessibility`). Open the "recent" tab.
2. **Reply genuinely to 5 posts.** Not "check out our app." A real reply, a laugh, an agreement, an answer. People click the profile of an account that said something good. That click is the whole game.
3. **Quote-share 1 post** from the community that fits the brand, with a short take of your own.
4. **Answer everyone** who replied to or commented on your posts. Every reply you give lifts that post in the algorithm and makes the next person more likely to join in.

That is it. Fifteen minutes. Reply-first, not plug-first.

## Tagging and @-mentions (do it right, or it backfires)

Tagging works when it is **relevant and occasional**. It gets you muted, reported, or shadowbanned when it is scattergun. A brand-new dating-app account mass-tagging strangers looks exactly like spam to the platform, and X and Meta will quietly throttle you for it, which is the opposite of what we want. So:

- **Tag an account only in a post that is genuinely about or relevant to them.** One or two a week, tops.
- **Never mass-tag strangers** hoping they notice. That is the fastest way to get the account limited.
- **Better than tagging: reply to their post.** A thoughtful reply on a big account's thread reaches their audience without tripping any spam filter.

### Accounts and communities worth engaging (confirm the exact handle before tagging)

Handles change, so search each name and check it is the real, active account before you tag. Engage first (reply, follow), tag later only when a post truly fits.

- **UK disability orgs:** Disability Rights UK (Instagram `@disrights_uk`), Scope, Leonard Cheshire, Disability Horizons. Good for accessibility and disclosure posts. Engage, don't spam; big orgs rarely reshare a commercial app, so the value is reaching *their audience* via replies.
- **Disability creators and advocates:** search `#disabilitycreator`, `#disabledandproud`, `#spoonie`, `#actuallyautistic` and find people with engaged (not just large) followings who post about dating, access, or daily life. Follow them, reply for a week, *then* consider a genuine collab ask by DM. Creators reshare; big charities usually don't.
- **Local Manchester:** `#Manchester`, `#GreaterManchester`, local LGBT+ and disability groups, accessible-venue accounts. Good for the Nearby / Plan-our-date posts.
- **Deaf community:** `#deafcommunity`, `#BSL` for the disclosure and video-chat angles.

## What NOT to do (these get the account throttled or banned)

- Mass @-mentioning strangers, or the same accounts repeatedly.
- Posting the identical text over and over (vary it, the app filters duplicates).
- Follow/unfollow churn to farm follows.
- DMing strangers with the app link. On a **dating** app account this reads as predatory and gets you reported fast.
- Stuffing 20+ hashtags, especially on X.

Aggressive is fine. Spammy gets you silenced. The line is: **add something to the conversation, or start one. Don't just shout your link.**

## Beyond the feed (bigger reach, when you have the energy)

- **Short video / Reels / TikTok.** Static cards cap out fast; short video is where disability creators actually reach large audiences. You are a performer, this is your home turf. A 20-second piece to camera about *why* you built it will out-reach any card.
- **Reddit:** r/disability, r/ChronicIllness, r/dating and local subs. Be a member first, share the story where it genuinely helps, follow each sub's self-promo rules.
- **Press:** local Manchester press and disability outlets love a founder-with-a-story launch. A short pitch email (see `05-aso-and-press-kit.md`) can do more than a month of posts.
