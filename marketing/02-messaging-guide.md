# Messaging & Voice Guide

The brand voice is the moat. Get this right and the rest of the marketing writes itself.

## Voice in three words
**Warm. Real. Confident.** We talk like a friend who's been there — not a brand, not a charity, not a clinician.

## Brand colors (from the app)
- Primary pink `#E91E63`
- Purple accent `#9C27B0`
- Like/success green `#4CAF50`
Keep marketing visuals consistent with the in-app palette so the ad-to-store-to-app journey feels like one thing.

## Taglines (approved)
- **You belong here.** *(primary)*
- Love, without the disclaimer.
- Where "so, there's something I should tell you" isn't a thing.
- Dating that gets it.
- Your story is a green flag.

## Messaging pillars
1. **No more disclosure dread.** On other apps, disability is a nervous reveal. Here it's just part of who you are — shared on your terms, among people who get it.
2. **Built with, not for.** Designed alongside disabled users. Accessibility is the foundation, not a settings toggle.
3. **Real connection, real people.** This is about dating and relationships — the fun, hopeful, human parts. We keep it light where it should be light.
4. **You set the terms.** Disclosure is optional and opt-in. You control what you share and when.

## Founder voice (read marketing/08-founder-voice.md first)

The brand speaks in the founder's voice: a Manchester comedy writer who is a full-time wheelchair user. Three extra hard rules from him:
1. **UK English** in everything public.
2. **No em dashes, ever.** Commas, brackets, colons or full stops.
3. **No AI-cliché words:** passionate, journey, empowering, inspiring, unique, transformative.
And the audience framing: Able2Love connects **disabled and non-disabled people open to dating one another**. Ordinary attraction, choice and confidence, not vulnerability.

## Language rules (non-negotiable)

### Do
- Use identity-first or person-first per the individual's preference; when writing broadly, mirror how the community you're addressing self-describes.
- Say **disabled people** / **the disability community** plainly. It is not a bad word.
- Center agency: people *choose*, *date*, *decide*, *want*.
- Keep it hopeful and often funny. Dating is supposed to be fun.

### Don't
- ❌ "Suffers from," "afflicted," "confined to a wheelchair," "special needs," "differently abled," "handicapable." (A wheelchair is freedom, not confinement.)
- ❌ Inspiration porn: "overcame," "despite her disability," "inspiring us all."
- ❌ Pity framing, sad-music-over-slow-motion, charity-telethon tone.
- ❌ Fetishization or anything that treats disability as a kink category.
- ❌ Speaking *for* the community in a claims-y way we can't back up. When in doubt, quote real members.

### The cringe test
Before anything ships, ask: *Would a disabled person forward this to a friend because it's good — or screenshot it to a group chat because it's bad?* If there's any doubt, a disabled reviewer on the team makes the call. Hire disabled marketers and creators; do not guess.

## Accessibility of the marketing itself
Practice what we preach:
- **Alt text on every image**, always. Templates in `04-social-content-pack.md`.
- **Captions/subtitles on every video** (open captions preferred on social).
- High color contrast (WCAG AA min) in all graphics.
- Avoid rapid flashing; respect reduced-motion.
- Don't put essential info only in emoji or only in an image.

## Proof points to repeat
- Optional, opt-in disclosure — you're in control.
- Accessible by design (screen-reader friendly, high-contrast, large-target UI).
- A community with shared understanding baked into the norms.
